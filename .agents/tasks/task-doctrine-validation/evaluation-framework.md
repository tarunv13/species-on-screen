# Experience Evaluation Framework

> One-time methodology for the first milestone review walk and the
> doctrine calibration that follows it.
>
> **Not steering. Not doctrine. Not governance.** This document is
> expected to be retired — or reduced to a brief postmortem entry —
> after the walk is complete and doctrine is calibrated. It does not
> bind future PRs.
>
> **Operating assumption:** the greatest risk to this project is not
> technical failure. It is the construction of an aesthetically
> sophisticated interface that fails to deepen ecological attention.
> Every section below is written against that risk.

---

## Section 1 — Target Perceptual States

States are described in terms of perceptual consequence (what the
viewer's attention is doing, what their body is doing, what
duration they are registering), not aesthetics. Designing for
aesthetics produces aesthetics; designing for perceptual
consequence produces ecology.

### Primary states (the project fails without these)

**P1. Temporal slowing.**
The viewer's internal clock decelerates. Operational signature:
they stop noticing clock-time. They look up after some interval and
have no idea how long it has been. Their breathing pattern changes
(slower, deeper). They do not check other tabs; they do not pick up
their phone. Attention has been claimed without the claiming being
felt as imposition.

**P2. Spatial humility.**
The viewer registers that they are small. The Earth is the subject;
they are not. The species is the subject; they are not. Operational
signature: a felt sense that what is on screen exists independently
of being looked at — that turning away would not stop the world
inside it. This is the inverse of game presence, which centres the
viewer as protagonist.

**P3. Ecological attention.**
Sustained looking at non-human subjects without that looking being
mediated by human-narrative scaffolding. Operational signature: the
eye stays on a habitat photograph after the caption has been read.
The viewer looks at the shape of the river before reading the
threat sentence. They re-read the threat sentence not to understand
it but to sit with it.

**P4. Observational intimacy.**
Proximity to a *specific* living thing as a specific living thing,
not as a representative of its taxon. Operational signature: the
viewer thinks "this tiger" rather than "tigers." They retain the
name of the place. The relationship is closer to acquaintance than
to information.

### Secondary states (emerge from primaries; should never be designed for directly)

**S1. Reverence.** Felt response to ecological scale and depth of
time. Earned, not produced. Designing for reverence directly
produces solemnity-aesthetic, which is itself a failure state.

**S2. Curiosity.** The wish to know more, generated by what has
been deliberately *not* shown. Operational signature: the viewer
follows a citation link unprompted, because they want the source.

**S3. Ecological grief.** Felt registration of loss. The most
ethically loaded state. Must be produced honestly (through the
threat sentences and IUCN status, in editorial register) and never
manipulatively (tragic music, swelling visuals, emotive copy).

**S4. Discomfort.** The productive friction of recognising one's
own complicity (consumer choices, geographic distance, prior
ignorance). Critically: this is not shame. Shame closes attention;
discomfort opens it. The threat sentence and the cultural paradox
are the doctrinal locations where discomfort is invited.

**S5. Narrative immersion.** Being held by the editorial line of a
single species' story without being conscious of the holding.
Operational signature: the viewer scrolls without remembering
deciding to scroll.

### Incompatible states (presence indicates failure)

**I1. Mastery / competence satisfaction** — "I understand this
system." If the viewer says "I get it" about the *interface*
rather than about the species, the interface has revealed itself.

**I2. Completion satisfaction** — "I've seen them all." The
species set is not a checklist; the satisfaction of having
finished it is a failure signature.

**I3. Tourism reward** — "I've been to the Sundarbans now." The
descent must not flatter the viewer into feeling they have visited
a place. They have looked. Looking is not visiting.

**I4. Comfort.** Sustained ease across a session. Conservation copy
that produces no discomfort has failed editorially. A perfectly
comfortable experience is a perfectly forgotten experience.

**I5. Aesthetic gratification as terminal state.** Beauty enjoyed
for itself, decoupled from what it depicts. This is the most
subtle failure mode and the one the project is most exposed to.

**I6. Flow / consumption velocity.** The sustained-engagement state
where one beat leads compulsively to the next. The doctrine
resists this through Holds and reading-time scroll governors, but
flow can re-emerge if cinematic transitions become too pleasurable.

**I7. Resolution / hope.** Closure on a species' situation. Hope is
not the doctrine's job; it is the viewer's response if any. The
interface must not deliver it.

## Section 2 — Failure States

For each: what it is, why it endangers ecological attention, how it
could emerge from the current doctrine. Numbered for citation in
walk notes.

### F1. Dashboard cognition

**What.** Reading the interface as a control panel for browsing a
dataset of species.

**Why dangerous.** Converts species from subjects to entries;
converts looking into filtering. Defeats P3 directly.

**How it could emerge from the doctrine.** The layer toggle bar
(Cinematic Article XII) drifts here if toggles are visually
prominent, if multi-select is implemented, or if any copy frames
layers as "filters." The danger zone is highest with 4+ visible
species at once, where the eye reads them as a list.

### F2. Gamified interaction

**What.** Interface delivers rewards (visual flourish, stat reveal,
"discovered" feedback) in response to actions.

**Why dangerous.** Recruits dopamine pathways that are
incompatible with ecological attention. The viewer attends to the
next-reward, not to the species.

**How it could emerge from the doctrine.** Pacing X's "held
luminance bloom on the visited hotspot" is the riskiest concrete
element. Calibration of its intensity and duration is the line
between "memory beat" and "achievement tick." A descent that is
too pleasurable in its Settle phase, relative to the Holds before
it, also drifts here.

### F3. Spectacle fatigue

**What.** Sensory saturation that produces affective numbing. Each
beat must be *more* to register; eventually nothing registers.

**Why dangerous.** First species feels overwhelming, fifth feels
nothing. Ecological grief becomes unavailable.

**How it could emerge from the doctrine.** Bloom intensity,
particle density, parallax depth, biome saturation, descent
grandeur — each species' safari restages the cinematic peak. With
ten species the viewer is asked to peak ten times. The doctrine
budgets *within* a beat (Pacing XVIII) but does not budget
*across* the session. This is a real gap.

### F4. Netflix browsing

**What.** Globe view becomes a content menu. Selection is the
reward; engagement is the obligation.

**Why dangerous.** Trains the viewer to optimise selection rather
than commit attention. Most species cards are looked at and
dismissed without descent.

**How it could emerge from the doctrine.** Floating glass cards on
a dark globe with a curated set of subjects is structurally
identical to a streaming service's home screen. Cinematic restraint
is what keeps them out of this register; if their copy becomes any
more "pitchy" the line collapses. The visited-bloom (Pacing X) is
a second risk: it converts the globe into a "continue watching"
surface.

### F5. Tourism aesthetics

**What.** Habitats are presented as desirable destinations. Beauty
without complicity. The implicit message becomes "look how rich
the natural world remains."

**Why dangerous.** Inverts the project's editorial intent. The
viewer leaves having seen beauty, not having registered loss.

**How it could emerge from the doctrine.** Biome palettes
(Article VII) are by definition the recognisable beauty of each
habitat. The three-layer parallax of the safari hero (Article XIII)
is a postcardable composition. If the threat sentence and cultural
paradox sit too far down the page, or render in a register that
competes poorly with the photograph, tourism wins.

### F6. Apple-commercial fetishization

**What.** The visual register of luxury technology advertising —
restraint, glass, atmospheric photography, smooth motion, generous
negative space, deliberate slowness — applied to ecological
subjects. The form *is* the message.

**Why dangerous.** This is the project's single most likely failure
mode and the hardest to detect, because the visuals look correct
against the cinematic doctrine. Every Article in
`cinematic-vocabulary.md` can be satisfied while producing a
fundamentally Apple-commercial result. The viewer leaves with the
felt sense of having watched a beautiful object.

**How it could emerge from the doctrine.** It emerges by default.
Restraint produces this register *unless* the editorial layer
(Canons V, VII, XI, XVIII) is visibly load-bearing in every beat.
The cinematic vocabulary alone is insufficient. The plain-screen
test (Section 5) exists to detect this.

### F7. Awwwards-style interaction vanity

**What.** Cleverness of interaction as a subject in itself. The
descent is admired *for being a descent*; the bloom is admired
*for being a bloom*.

**Why dangerous.** The technology becomes the protagonist.
Ecological attention is impossible because attention is on the
apparatus.

**How it could emerge from the doctrine.** The CinematicEngine and
the Descent are technically interesting. First-time technical
interest is acceptable; if it persists into the third species, the
apparatus has won.

### F8. Compulsive novelty loops

**What.** Pattern of rapid descent → return → next species →
descent → return. Each species is "previewed," not seen.

**Why dangerous.** Ten species visited in three minutes is not
ecological attention; it is compulsive sampling.

**How it could emerge from the doctrine.** If returning to the
globe is too pleasurable (warm-cards stagger of Pacing X), if the
descent is shorter than the doctrinal envelope, or if the viewer
learns that Return is friction-free, novelty-seeking becomes the
dominant strategy.

### F9. Interaction anxiety

**What.** Hesitation born of fear that interaction will commit the
viewer to something undesirable. "If I click this card, am I
trapped in a 30-second cutscene?"

**Why dangerous.** Suppresses exploration. Viewer never enters a
descent. The local-time register is never reached.

**How it could emerge from the doctrine.** The 3-second descent
envelope (Pacing III) and the no-skip rule are necessary for the
cinematic contract but produce anxiety in viewers conditioned by
dismissable modals. The signal of which click triggers a descent
versus a card-expand must be unambiguous; ambiguity here produces
freeze.

### F10. Cinematic excess detached from ecological meaning

**What.** Beautiful execution that the viewer experiences as art,
not as ecology. The Sundarbans look stunning and the viewer thinks
about the rendering, not about the tigers.

**Why dangerous.** The umbrella failure. Most other failure states
are special cases of this one.

**How it could emerge from the doctrine.** Anywhere the cinematic
doctrine outpaces the editorial doctrine. Anywhere the visual
budget is spent before the editorial budget is. The single
highest-leverage defence is Canon XVIII (COM-B claims with
sources): if the viewer reads even one COM-B claim and follows its
citation, the experience shifts from cinema to inquiry.

## Section 3 — Observable Behavioral Signals

Signals are stratified by what they cost the project to read. Some
can be observed during walks (qualitative). A small subset can be
measured during walks (quantitative). Some can only be inferred
after the fact. Some must not be measured at all, because
measurement would corrupt the experience.

### Qualitative — success signals

- Viewer goes silent during the first hold of a descent.
- Viewer leans toward the screen at the bottom of a parallax beat,
  not away.
- Viewer scrolls back up after reading a threat sentence
  (re-reading).
- Viewer pauses with the cursor not moving for ≥15 seconds.
- After closing the tab, the viewer asks a question — about the
  species or place, not about the interface.
- Viewer remembers the place name a day later, unprompted.
- Viewer mentions complicity (palm oil, fisheries, climate) in
  unprompted debrief.
- A second walker, walking independently, lingers at the same
  beats as the first.

### Qualitative — failure signals

- Viewer comments on visual quality before commenting on the
  species.
- Viewer asks "how did you do that?" about the descent before
  asking anything about the place.
- Viewer scrolls past a threat sentence without visible
  registration.
- Viewer treats the globe as a menu — eye tracking moves
  systematically left-to-right or pin-to-pin without dwell.
- Viewer enters and exits multiple species in under two minutes
  each (sampling).
- Viewer reads citation links to evaluate the project's
  credibility, not to follow the source.
- Viewer says "cool" or "nice." These are tourism vocabulary, not
  ecological vocabulary.
- Viewer asks about features ("can I save this?", "is there a list
  view?") — interface as object.

### Quantitative — permitted only inside walks, never as KPIs

These are admitted because they signal *failure modes*, not
engagement. Surfacing them as ongoing telemetry would convert
them into optimisation targets and would violate Pacing XX.

| Measurement | Walk target | Failure threshold | What failure indicates |
|---|---|---|---|
| Time spent in first hold of a descent | Full doctrinal value preserved | <400ms | Viewer interpreted hold as wait-state |
| Ratio of descent-entries to descent-completions | ≥0.95 | <0.85 | Anxiety (F9) or boredom |
| Species visited per 10-minute walk | 1–3 | ≥5 | Novelty loop (F8) |
| Time between successive species selections | ≥3 min | <90 s | Netflix browsing (F4) |
| Scroll velocity inside safari | Within ±30% of reading-time table | Above range | Scroll-through, comprehension failure |

### Forbidden to measure — even in walks

- Time-on-page, time-on-site (Pacing XX, but also: meaningless
  without context).
- Click-through rate from card to descent.
- Bounce rate.
- Anything that could become a dashboard tile if surfaced.

### Inferred — read indirectly from artefacts of the walk

- **24-hour recall test.** A day after the walk, the observer
  writes down everything they remember without referring to notes.
  High recall of place names + low recall of interface details =
  ecological attention. Inverse pattern = apparatus dominance.
- **Emotional register of recall.** "I keep thinking about" vs.
  "it was very well done." The first signals the project
  succeeding; the second signals F6.
- **Behavioural change at the margin.** Did the walker, in their
  own time, look up palm oil, look up the Sundarbans, follow a
  citation link unprompted? Even one such action across a small
  walk cohort is a strong success signal.
- **Reluctance to leave.** Closing quickly is a weak failure signal
  (could be neutral). Closing and re-opening a day later, without
  prompt, is a strong success signal.

### Should NOT be measured — measurement would corrupt the experience

- **Anything exposed to the viewer.** Counters, "X minutes
  watched," "X% explored." The act of showing these creates
  collection-completion satisfaction (I2).
- **Anything that becomes a target if shown to the project.** The
  conversion rate from card-hover to descent, surfaced as a metric,
  produces designs that maximise it. Those designs optimise for
  novelty seeking, which is the failure state being measured. This
  is the canonical case of a metric corrupting its subject.
- **Emotional response to specific beats.** Real and important, but
  not measurable without interview, and interview that focuses on
  emotion produces performative responses. Better read through
  behaviour at the margin.
- **Aesthetic-preference ratings.** "Did you like the descent?"
  asks the wrong question. The right question is "what do you
  remember about the Sundarbans?"
- **NPS, satisfaction, engagement scores.** Ban with prejudice.

## Section 4 — Ecological Attention as Interaction Design

Operational practice that the doctrine implies. Translates the
abstract goal into concrete rules that can be checked during a
walk and during PR review. Not new doctrine; not steering;
falsifiable practice.

### 4.1 Information density thresholds

- **One claim per beat.** A beat carries: one observation, OR one
  stat-as-sentence, OR one threat paragraph, OR one cultural
  paradox, OR one citation cluster. Never two of these.
- **Photographs are claims.** A photograph and a stat in the same
  beat is over-density.
- **Maximum visible text per beat:** ~60 words on mobile, ~120 on
  desktop. Target reading time per beat: 25–50 seconds.
- **Maximum interactive elements per beat:** one.

### 4.2 Motion restraint principles

- **Default state of every element is at-rest.** Motion exists
  only at transition.
- **Ambient motion** (parallax drift, particle motion, globe
  rotation) is the *constant* state of the scene. It is not
  noticeable; it is breathing.
- **Editorial motion** (reveals, descents) occurs exactly once per
  beat.
- **Motion in response to passive signals** (cursor position,
  viewport scroll without intent) is forbidden, except where it is
  the constant ambient state.
- **Motion budget across a session:** target <15% of total session
  time spent in motion. Stillness is the dominant register.

### 4.3 Environmental scale communication

- **The Earth must always read as larger than the chrome around
  it.** Any frame that visually constrains its scale is wrong.
- **The species silhouette at full descent must not be larger in
  the frame than the photograph that follows it.** The species is
  bigger than its representation; the interface should not flatter
  the representation.
- **The viewer is never the largest implied entity in any frame.**
  No first-person presence.
- **Habitat photographs include scale references** (a tree, a
  horizon, a vehicle, a person at distance) that establish the
  place is larger than what is shown.

### 4.4 Whitespace behaviour

- **Whitespace is time, not space.** The mandatory Holds (Pacing V)
  and inter-beat pauses are the project's whitespace. Pixel
  whitespace serves it.
- **A composition with crowded text and generous pixel whitespace
  is not in voice if its temporal pacing is compressed.** A
  composition with dense text and adequate temporal pacing is in
  voice.
- **The look-away test.** The viewer should be able to look away
  for ten seconds and return to find nothing has happened. This is
  the test of correct whitespace behaviour.

### 4.5 Transition cadence logic

- **Transitions occupy time that would otherwise carry content.**
  They are the editorial decision to *not* show something.
- **Transition duration is proportional to scale change.** Bigger
  scale change → longer transition. The Descent is long because it
  crosses planetary→local; a layer toggle is short.
- **Every transition is followed by a hold.** The hold is what
  makes the transition legible as a transition rather than a
  glitch.
- **Two transitions in a row (no hold between) produce
  disorientation.** Forbidden.

### 4.6 Temporal pacing rules

- **The minimum unit of pacing is the beat.** Beats are editorial,
  not metric.
- **Within a session, rhythm must vary.** The descent is
  fast-then-slow. The safari is slow throughout. The return is
  medium. Variation prevents the experience from settling into a
  tempo the viewer can game.
- **The viewer's pace overrides the system's pace** wherever both
  are defined. Reading time wins over animation timing.

### 4.7 Interaction friction philosophy

- **Friction is information.** A click triggering a 3-second
  composed transition communicates "this is a commitment." That
  communication is a feature.
- **Friction must be legible.** The viewer must understand,
  *before* clicking, what scale of commitment a click represents.
  Mystery friction is hostile; legible friction is respectful.
- **Reversibility is not advertised.** The viewer should know
  Return is possible (Article XIV); they should not be told this
  every time they enter a safari.
- **Undo is not a goal.** Many actions in this experience are not
  undoable in the sense the term is usually used (you cannot
  un-see the threat sentence). The interface must not pretend
  otherwise.

### 4.8 Anti-dopaminergic interaction design

- **No element produces a positive sensory response to a viewer's
  action.** A click feels composed, not rewarding.
- **Hover states are functional, not emotional.** They communicate
  "this is interactive"; they do not delight.
- **Sound, when implemented (Article XVI), follows this rule
  absolutely.** No interface sound. No click feedback. No
  notification chimes. Sound is environmental, not transactional.
- **No counters, fillings, level-ups, or escalating-reward
  structures of any kind across a session.**
- **The interface does not say "thank you."** It acknowledges
  actions; it does not appreciate them.

### 4.9 Ambient vs. directive interface behaviour

- **The interface waits. It does not direct.**
- **No first-time-user experience, no onboarding flow, no hint
  system, no guided tour.**
- **No prompts about elements the viewer has not engaged with.**
  No "you haven't visited this species yet."
- **No explanation of itself.** First session and hundredth session
  look identical from the system's side; only the viewer changes.
- **The single permitted directive behaviour is editorial:** the
  documentary narrator may, very rarely, situate the viewer in the
  place ("here, the canopy thins" — Canon I). This is observation,
  not instruction.

## Section 5 — Live Review Walk Methodology

### 5.1 Pre-walk preparation

**Environment.**

- Walk in a single uninterrupted block of ≥30 minutes.
- Walk on the *deployed* site, not localhost. The cinematic
  doctrine assumes the experience is encountered through a URL.
- Walk on a baseline device (mid-range mobile, cellular network if
  possible) AND on a high-end desktop. Two passes minimum.
- Walk in moderate ambient lighting. Not a dark room (flatters
  cinematic visuals). Not bright daylight (flattens them). The
  doctrine should hold under normal conditions.
- No caffeine in the 90 minutes prior. Caffeine raises baseline
  arousal and corrupts the temporal-slowing observation (P1).
- Phone in another room. The walk requires uninterrupted attention
  as the primary instrument.

**Doctrine priming.**

- Re-read the three steering documents within 24 hours before the
  walk, then *do not* re-read within 4 hours of the walk. Doctrine
  must be available to memory but not active in working memory.
- Bring a printed list of Articles, Canons, and Principles by name
  only (no body text). Citations during the walk are by name;
  lookups happen after.

**Self-state.**

- Walk fed but not full, rested but not under-slept, in a neutral
  mood. The walk is an attention test; attention is corrupted by
  extreme states in either direction.

### 5.2 Walkthrough sequence

Each stage has a goal and a duration. The observer's job is to
*observe the experience and themselves observing it*.

**Stage 1 — Cold landing (3 min).**
Open the URL. Do not interact for the first minute. Hold the
cursor away from the screen. Observe what the experience does
when no input is given. Note the immediate emotional register of
the first 10 seconds. The doctrine assumes contemplative; if the
first response is "what is this?" or "what do I do?", cold
landing has failed.

**Stage 2 — Free planetary exploration (5–7 min).**
Drag the globe; observe inertia and card behaviour during drag.
Hover hotspots; do not click yet. Toggle each layer once; observe
the transition; time the gap subjectively (beat or stutter?).
Note: at any point, did the apparatus reveal itself?

**Stage 3 — Single descent, full attention.**
Choose a species the viewer is naturally drawn to. Do not choose
strategically. Click. Observe the entire descent without
scrolling, without moving the cursor. Sit in the safari hero for
≥30 seconds before scrolling. Note what is observed during that
time, and *what is not observed* — which elements never appeared
in attention.

**Stage 4 — Full descent, all beats, viewer's pace.**
Read every word. Read the COM-B claims. Read the threat sentences.
Read captions. Click at least one citation link and read the
source for ≥60 seconds. Note: which beats produced ecological
attention? Which felt like pages of a website? Did the cultural
paradox produce paradox or smoothing? Did any threat sentence
produce discomfort (S4)? Did any photograph produce tourism (F5)?

**Stage 5 — Return.**
Use the Return affordance. Did the return feel like ascent or
like closing a modal? What is the state of the globe on return?
Does the visited bloom feel like memory or like a check-mark (F2)?

**Stage 6 — Second descent, different species.**
Note: is the second descent felt as familiar or as repetitive? At
what point does the apparatus become invisible? At what point
does the apparatus become irritating? Are the cards on the globe
reading more like a menu now than they did in Stage 2 (F4)?

**Stage 7 — Third descent (only if Stage 6 did not collapse into
novelty seeking).**
The walk's most diagnostic stage. By the third species, either
the doctrine is holding (the viewer attends to the third species
*more* than the first) or it is not (the viewer is sampling).

**Stage 8 — Close.**
Close the tab. Do not re-open. Sit for 5 minutes in silence.
Notes are written *after* the sit, not during. Whatever survives
the 5 minutes is the experience; whatever does not survive was
apparatus.

### 5.3 Observer note structure

For each stage, notes follow a fixed shape:

```
STAGE: [number — name]
DURATION: [actual time spent]

WHAT THE INTERFACE DID:
[observed behaviour, plain description, no interpretation]

WHAT I DID:
[viewer behaviour: hesitations, re-reads, scroll-backs]

WHAT I NOTICED I WAS FEELING:
[register: contemplative / curious / impatient / bored / anxious /
detached / moved / etc. Single words preferred.]

WHAT I DID NOT NOTICE UNTIL LATER:
[elements present in the frame that never received attention.
These are the most informative observations.]

DOCTRINE CITATIONS:
[Articles / Canons / Principles satisfied or violated, by name.
Body text not quoted; cross-referencing happens during calibration.]

CONTRADICTIONS:
[where doctrine and experience disagree — see 5.4]
```

### 5.4 Contradiction logging format

A contradiction is a moment where doctrine and experience
disagree. There are three kinds; each implies a different
response.

**Type A — Doctrine satisfied, experience fails.**
The Article/Canon/Principle was visibly upheld, and the experience
was nonetheless wrong.
*Implication:* doctrine is incomplete or wrongly framed.
*Action:* amend doctrine.

**Type B — Doctrine violated, experience succeeds.**
The implementation broke a rule, and the experience was
nonetheless right.
*Implication:* the rule is too tight or wrongly bounded.
*Action:* relax the rule, with the observation cited.

**Type C — Doctrine and experience aligned, value disagreed.**
The shape of the rule was right; the specific value (ms, vh,
ratio, threshold) was wrong.
*Implication:* parameter calibration needed.
*Action:* amend the value, cite the walk observation.

Each contradiction is logged as:

```
ID: C-[NN]
TYPE: A / B / C
LOCATION: stage and approximate timestamp
DOCTRINE: Article/Canon/Principle by name
OBSERVATION: 1–3 sentences, factual
PROPOSED RESPONSE: amendment / relaxation / calibration
URGENCY: must-resolve-before-prototype / can-defer
```

### 5.5 Doctrine calibration criteria

Not every contradiction warrants a doctrine change.

**A contradiction warrants amendment if:**
- Type A, and the implementation cannot be changed to satisfy
  doctrine without violating other doctrine. (The doctrine is
  genuinely self-contradictory.)
- Type B, and the same relaxation is observed in two independent
  walks.
- Type C, and the proposed value sits outside ±20% of the
  doctrinal value. Smaller deltas are calibration noise.

**A contradiction does not warrant amendment if:**
- It can be resolved by changing the implementation without
  doctrine change.
- It was observed in only one walk.
- The amendment would weaken the doctrine in ways unrelated to
  the observation.
- The amendment would unblock a single PR but leave a vague rule
  for future PRs.

Calibration produces a single PR amending only flagged values. It
does not produce new Articles, Canons, or Principles. Numbers are
preserved; bodies are revised.

### 5.6 "Do not trust your own excitement" safeguards

The walk's hardest discipline is recognising aesthetic seduction.
The interface is designed to be beautiful; the observer is
designed to respond to beauty. The walk's truth depends on
separating beauty from ecological success.

**SG1. The 24-hour rule.**
No walk note is final until 24 hours have passed. The first hour
after the walk produces over-positive notes (afterglow) and
over-negative notes (post-hoc skepticism). Truth lives at 24
hours.

**SG2. The recall test.**
24 hours later, write down everything remembered without
referring to notes. What is remembered is what the experience is.
Target: place names, threat magnitudes, one or two specific
images, possibly one citation. Failure: dominant memory of how
the interface looked, how the descent felt, "the bloom on the
globe."

**SG3. The second observer.**
A second person walks the same path independently, with no
priming beyond the URL. Notes are compared after both have
completed. Convergent observations are signal; divergent
observations are calibration noise (or an interesting failure of
the doctrine to produce a consistent experience).

**SG4. The plain-screen test.**
After the walk, look at the deployed site for 10 seconds with
all editorial copy hidden (browser dev tools). What remains? If
what remains is a luxury technology advertisement, the editorial
layer is not load-bearing and the doctrine has failed
experientially even if every Article is satisfied. (Direct test
for F6.)

**SG5. The "what do I want to talk about?" test.**
Immediately after the walk, the observer notes what they want to
talk about with another person. If "the descent," "the globe
interaction," or "how the cards feel" — apparatus dominance. If
"the Sundarbans" or "what's happening to the polar bear" —
ecological attention.

**SG6. The excitement quarantine.**
Any walk note containing "stunning," "beautiful," "wow," "love,"
or any aesthetic-praise vocabulary is suspect. Re-read those
entries: am I praising the apparatus? If yes, the entry is
evidence of failure, not success.

**SG7. Walk on a bad day.**
The doctrine must hold when the observer is not predisposed to
wonder. One walk should be conducted on a day the observer is
tired, distracted, or in a flat mood. A doctrine that only works
on receptive viewers fails the project's purpose.

## Section 6 — Tiger / Sundarbans Prototype Readiness

### 6.1 Evidence that must exist before the prototype begins

1. A completed milestone walk against the deployed site (per
   Section 5), with notes, contradictions logged, and the 24-hour
   recall test completed (SG1, SG2).
2. A doctrine calibration PR merged — OR an explicit
   acknowledgement, in the calibration PR's stead, that the walk
   produced no amendment-warranting contradictions. The doctrine
   must be at rest.
3. A walk-derived list of which Articles, Canons, and Principles
   *failed* to produce their intended perceptual state in the
   existing experience. The prototype's primary purpose is to test
   whether terrain implementation can succeed where the existing
   experience could not.
4. A scoping document for the prototype, naming explicitly:
   - The single target species and place: Tiger, Sundarbans.
   - The Articles/Canons/Principles the prototype is exercising
     for the first time. Terrain rendering exercises (at minimum):
     Cinematic Articles III, V, VIII, XIII, XV; Pacing III, VIII,
     XV, XVI, XVIII.
   - The Articles/Canons/Principles the prototype is *explicitly
     not* exercising. Sound (Article XVI) is excluded. Multi-
     species cadence comparison is excluded. New COM-B research is
     excluded — existing tiger COM-B data is reused unchanged.
   - The reduced-motion path (Pacing XV) for the new descent,
     designed at the same time as the primary path, not retrofit.
   - Three to five named perceptual hypotheses the prototype is
     designed to test (see 6.3).
5. A baseline performance budget, measured against the deployed
   pre-prototype site: target framerate inside the descent on
   baseline mobile, target time-to-first-hold, target peak GPU
   memory. Operational floors, not metrics for promotion.
6. Confirmation that no other doctrinal documents or steering
   files are in flux at the moment the prototype branch is cut.
   Concurrent doctrine churn invalidates the test.

### 6.2 Unresolved ambiguities that would make implementation premature

If any of the following are unresolved at scoping time, the
scoping document must address them or the prototype must wait.

**A1. The grammar of terrain in the descent.**
The cinematic doctrine specifies that the Crossing happens inside
a luminance dip and that the safari's first beat is a hold on the
place (Article XIII). With terrain, "the place" is a 3D space.
Is the first hold a static composition (a held terrain frame), or
a slow ambient drift over terrain? Doctrine-level question, not
engineering. Must be resolved before implementation.

**A2. The status of map UI primitives.**
Terrain implies temptation toward map controls — compass, scale
bar, elevation indicator, GPS pin. The doctrine forbids GIS
register (Cinematic §0, Article XII; Editorial Canon XII). The
prototype must specify what is permitted in lieu of these
primitives. The answer cannot be "nothing" without a design — a
3D space with no orientation cues fails on usability.

**A3. The relationship between terrain detail and editorial
restraint.**
Higher terrain fidelity tends to produce more cinematic spectacle
(F3, F6). The prototype must specify the fidelity ceiling, and
the criteria for choosing between fidelity and restraint when
they conflict.

**A4. Sound's relationship to terrain.**
Article XVI reserves sound; terrain is the most sound-sensitive
surface in the project. The scoping document must explicitly
acknowledge that the prototype ships *without* sound, AND must
identify any rendering decisions (asset sourcing, scene
composition) that should anticipate eventual sound to avoid
retrofit.

**A5. The descent's destination ambiguity.**
Currently, a descent ends in a 2D safari scene. With terrain it
can end in a 3D scene the viewer can move through. The prototype
must answer: does the viewer have agency *inside* the terrain, or
is the terrain a composed 3D still that the camera moves through
on its own? This is a perceptual question (does interaction in
terrain produce ecological attention or undermine it?), and the
prototype is designed to answer it. Therefore the prototype must
have a working hypothesis going in.

**A6. The threshold of detail at which biome palette becomes
redundant.**
Article VII assumes biome palettes are authored. With photographic
terrain, the palette emerges from the terrain itself. The
doctrine has not yet specified whether the JSON `safari_scene`
palette governs terrain rendering, is overridden by it, or
coexists with it.

If any of A1–A6 is unresolved at scoping, the prototype is
premature. It is more useful for the doctrine to spend another
walk cycle resolving them than for the prototype to ship with
structural ambiguity.

### 6.3 Specific perceptual questions the first descent prototype is designed to answer

The prototype is not a feature build. It is an experiment. Its
outputs are observations, not features.

**Q1. Does terrain produce spatial humility (P2) more strongly
than the current safari hero?**
- *Hypothesis:* yes, because terrain communicates scale through
  depth.
- *Falsifier:* viewers comment on the technology of the terrain,
  not the scale of the place.

**Q2. Does terrain accelerate or slow the viewer's reading of the
editorial copy?**
- *Hypothesis:* it slows reading (the eye lingers on terrain
  before text).
- *Falsifier:* reading speed is unchanged or accelerated,
  suggesting terrain is decorative rather than perceptual.

**Q3. Does the descent into terrain feel like arrival or like a
cinematic cutscene?**
- *Hypothesis:* the difference depends on the Settle phase's
  composition. A held terrain frame produces arrival; an animated
  camera glide produces cutscene.
- *Falsifier:* viewers describe the experience as "watching"
  rather than "being in" the place. (Note: the project explicitly
  does not want first-person presence either; the target is
  "looking at," which is between watching and being-in.)

**Q4. Does the threat sentence land harder when read against
terrain than when read against parallax?**
- *Hypothesis:* yes, because the spatial register matches the
  editorial register.
- *Falsifier:* the threat sentence is *softer* against terrain,
  because the terrain's beauty competes with the threat. (This
  would be a major signal that the editorial layer must be made
  structurally more dominant.)

**Q5. Does the terrain implementation invite repeat descent of the
same species, or repeat exploration of the descended place?**
- *Hypothesis (target):* the latter. Viewers return to the
  Sundarbans because they want to look at the Sundarbans again,
  not because they want to perform the descent again.
- *Falsifier:* viewers re-trigger the descent itself for its
  cinematic effect — F7 (interaction vanity).

**Q6 (open).** Does the prototype, by the end of two walks,
suggest that terrain is the right grammar for *all* ten species'
descents, or only some?
- This question does not need an answer at prototype start; the
  prototype's purpose is to surface evidence. But the scoping
  document must commit, in advance, to letting the answer be
  either. A prototype that succeeds for the Sundarbans does not
  automatically generalise; Polar Bear or Blue Whale may need a
  different grammar entirely. The prototype must not be staffed
  or scoped with "we will then build this for all ten" as a
  foregone conclusion.

These questions are the prototype's success criteria. The
prototype succeeds if it produces evidence on each — including,
critically, evidence that the doctrine is wrong about one of them.
**Failure to falsify is a successful experiment.** A prototype
that confirms every hypothesis is suspect.

## Closing note

This framework expires on its own success. After the milestone
walk and the calibration PR, this document is reduced to a brief
postmortem entry summarising what was learned and what was
amended. It is not promoted to steering. It does not bind future
PRs. Its purpose is finished when the doctrine has been validated
once against live perception.

The framework's own success criterion is the one it imposes on the
project: did it deepen attention to what matters, or did it
produce another beautiful object?
