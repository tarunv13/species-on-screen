What We Observe

We trace the invisible threads between the animals that appear on our screens and the ecosystems they call home. Three lenses guide this observation.

🎥

Species on Screen

How species appear in human media and culture - films, documentaries, news coverage, social media, literature, and advertising. Every representation is a data point in the cultural record of how we see the living world.

🌱

Ecological Reality

What is actually happening to these species in the wild. Population trends, habitat loss, threat assessments, and conservation status paint a picture that often diverges sharply from the stories we tell on screen.

🔗

The Connection

How cultural representation may shape conservation outcomes. Does screen attention translate to conservation action? Does media portrayal match ecological truth? These are the questions the observatory exists to explore.

Species Profiles

Ten flagship species observed through the lens of cinema and ecology. Each profile explores the gap between cultural visibility and conservation reality.

Every Story Shapes Our Response

The Eco-Cinema Observatory now profiles 10 flagship species across tropical forests, oceans, mountains, arctic tundra, savanna, and coral reef ecosystems. By mapping the connections between cultural representation and ecological reality, we are building a foundation to understand how human attention relates to species fate.
